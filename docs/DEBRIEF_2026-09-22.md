# Debrief — AI Grand Prix, 2026-09-22 — why we did not pass a gate

Seven autonomous attempts across two slots on two days. Zero gates. This is
the honest account of why, written from the flight logs, ranked by how much
each cause cost us.

---

## The one-line answer

We flew a stack whose vertical channel had never been proven on the
aircraft, against a course we could not practise on, and we found out what
was wrong one attempt at a time, in the only minutes that counted.

## Why we failed, in order of cost

### 1. The aircraft has no vertical speed it can trust, and the design needed one

The vertical channel steers on the gate's elevation, which is right until
the ring clips the frame at about 3.5 m. From there to the gate the design
holds height blind for three seconds on a vertical speed. We tried three
sources for that speed:

- **Barometer:** +-0.4 m/s of noise in flight, and about 1 m high after
  takeoff. At 200 us per m/s of it, one phantom reading moved him 0.7 m.
- **Accelerometer over MSP at 32 Hz:** reads about 0.35 m/s^2 low under the
  props (aliased vibration). Its integral drifts 0.3 m/s every second.
- **Vision fit of the elevation history:** correct until the ring clips,
  which is exactly when we need it.

None of these survives three seconds. The "commit then fly blind" design
was built on an assumption the airframe cannot meet. That is the root cause.
Every other item below is how we found it out slowly.

### 2. A wrong hover throttle sat in the config for four attempts

`hover_pwm = 1228` was measured on d45 two days earlier. Julian hovers at
1205. Every "hold height" was a 23 us climb, every "descend" was a hover.
Attempt 3's log shows it plainly: four seconds of "EASING DOWN" and the
barometer flat. A ten-minute hover test on Julian, any day this week, would
have found it. We never did one, because we never had a place to fly.

### 3. The simulator could not see any of this

The day before the slot went into the Betaflight SITL: ANGLE mode, sensor
noise, a baro model, seed sweeps. It reached 23 of 23 gates. It cannot model
accelerometer aliasing over MSP, the barometer's takeoff transient, a ring
clipped by the frame, or the airframe's hover point. It found and fixed
problems at the stacked gate and the hairpin while the real failure was the
first gate's vertical. The 23/23 gave a confidence that was not earned. I
drove that loop and reported that confidence.

### 4. Every attempt flew code that had never flown

Between attempts the build changed, sometimes by several things at once:
a new vertical-speed source, a new hold, new switches. Two of seven attempts
were lost outright to brand-new bugs (the pad commit in 4, the hold release
in 7) that a bench run with a red object in front of the camera, or a
release-on-time rule from the start, would have caught. The other attempts
each proved one thing and could not prove the next. With one aircraft, one
slot and no practice, that is one flight per fact. We needed all the facts
before the slot.

### 5. No practice, no gate, no bench

The rules gave us the start line only inside the slot. We had no gate of our
own, no hover flights, no way to fly an approach outside the 27 minutes.
Everything we knew about the aircraft came from the slot itself. A course
this short rewards the team that arrives with a flown stack; we arrived with
a simulated one.

### 6. Process on the day

- Attempt 4's PAD line read "gate at 1.8 m, below". The card said "about
  7.5 m, ABOVE". It was armed anyway. The check existed; it was not the
  habit.
- The flight commands grew to five environment switches. Correct each time,
  but a fragile way to run a race.
- The pilot's abort cue "rising after COMMIT" was right, and the rise was
  too fast to act on.

## What the photos of the strike show

Two stills from the slot, the aircraft at the right inner edge of g0 about
a third of the way down the opening, and at the top edge near the left
corner.

- **The opening is about 1.4 m square inside a 2.7 m panel.** Half a metre
  of clearance each side for a 0.35 m aircraft. "0.2 m low and on the line"
  was the edge of the window, not the middle of it.
- **The panel is a wide red square with white lettering, logos and checker
  patterns inside the red.** The HSV mask sees the white as holes. The
  blob's centroid and bounding box are not the hole's centre, by tens of
  centimetres, and the offset changes with viewing angle. Never measured,
  because we never had a gate to look at.
- **Detect the hole, not the red.** The opening's four inner edges are
  straight lines on a flat panel: the cleanest PnP target there is, and it
  is measured against the thing he has to fit through.

## What would have passed g0

By attempt 6 the stack had: measured hover, elevation steering with
aim-low, the lateral on the gate's line, release on time, and a commit at
3.6 m. What it lacked was a height hold for three seconds. The unflown
candidate is the hover-throttle hold with a light trim. It might have made
g0. It is still a guess, and the guess is the problem.

The real fix is a sensor or a signal that gives height through the crossing:
a downward rangefinder or optical flow, or tracking the ring's top edge
until the aircraft is through it. That is a build, not a switch.

## What we keep

- The takeoff, release, approach and lateral are flight-proven now.
- Julian's hover: 1205 us. The accelerometer and barometer numbers above.
- The detector, the map, the plan, the referee-style crossing count.
- Seven annotated flight logs and a sim that runs the whole course in ANGLE
  mode, useful for everything except the vertical.

Everything is on `sim-angle-loop` with each flight's cause in its commit.
